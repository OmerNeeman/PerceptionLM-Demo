PerceptionLM for Aerial Tracking

A review of Meta's PerceptionLM (PLM) and Perception Encoder (PE), and the practical options for using them to track objects in aerial images and video — offline analysis context.

Executive summary — the one-paragraph decision

PerceptionLM is not a tracker; it is an open vision-language model that understands images and video in detail. In the paper, tracks are an input it describes, produced upstream by SAM 2. For our aerial use case the best value-for-effort path is therefore a two-model pipeline: PLM finds the target from a natural-language description (a bounding box on one frame), SAM 2 propagates that box as a tracked mask through the video. Zero training, off-the-shelf checkpoints, runs offline on a single 24–80 GB GPU. PLM then adds what no plain tracker gives us: per-track natural-language analysis ("when does this vehicle stop?"). The known risk is domain gap — the models were trained on web/egocentric video, not overhead imagery — so step one of future work is a small aerial evaluation set; the upgrade path if zero-shot quality falls short is fine-tuning a detector on VisDrone with the released PE Spatial backbone.

Part I

Paper review

What the paper contributes

PerceptionLM: Open-Access Data and Models for Detailed Visual Understanding (Cho et al., Meta FAIR) is primarily a statement about openness. Most strong vision-language models are either closed, or open-weight but trained by distilling from closed models, which makes the results scientifically unreproducible. PLM is trained end-to-end without any distillation from proprietary models, and everything is released: weights, training data recipe, and evaluation suite.

The concrete releases matter more to us than the ideology:

  • PLM models at 1B, 3B and 8B parameters (research license).
  • Perception Encoder (PE), the vision backbone family, released separately under Apache 2.0 (companion paper, arXiv 2504.13181).
  • 2.8M human-labeled video instances: fine-grained video QA (PLM-FGQA) and spatio-temporally grounded captions (PLM-STC) — the largest such release to date.
  • PLM-VideoBench, a benchmark for fine-grained and temporally grounded video understanding.

Architecture in numbers

1B / 3B / 8BModel sizes; PE vision encoder → 2-layer MLP → Llama 3 decoder
448 × 448 pxResolution per image tile and per video frame
36 tilesDynamic tiling for high-res images (up to 9,472 vision tokens)
32 framesUniformly sampled per video, no tiling (8,192 vision tokens)

Each 448² tile or frame produces 1,024 vision tokens, reduced to 256 by 2×2 average pooling. The asymmetry in the last two tiles above is the single most important fact for aerial work: images get high-resolution tiling; video does not. A small vehicle that survives 36-tile image processing disappears when the whole frame is resized to 448² on the video path.

The three region tasks — and where the tracks come from

The paper's "spatio-temporal grounding" is what makes it sound like a tracking paper. Precisely, PLM learns three region-conditioned tasks:

  • RCap — given a box/mask region on a frame, caption that region.
  • RTLoc — given a region and an event description, output the start/end frames.
  • RDCap — dense captioning of everything a region does across the clip.
The key clarification

In all three tasks the region is an input. To build the PLM-STC training data, Meta ran SAM 2 to generate masklets (tracked segmentation masks) and had humans caption them. PLM never learns to output a track — it learns to reason about one. Any tracking system built on this paper is therefore a pipeline: something produces the track, PLM interprets it (or seeds it, via image grounding).

On the image side PLM additionally does grounding — it outputs a bounding box for a text query (the repo ships an image_grounding.ipynb demo). That single capability is the hook that lets PLM act as an open-vocabulary detector.

Perception Encoder: the reusable half

PE is a family of ViT encoders with one training recipe and three "views", exposed by taking features from different depths. For us, PE Spatial is the interesting one — it is explicitly refined with a SAM 2.1 mask-based objective for dense prediction, and it is the released component you would build a real aerial detector on.

The 61.5 J&F on DAVIS is zero-shot video object tracking by non-parametric feature propagation — no tracking head was trained. It signals that PE Spatial features carry the temporal correspondence structure a tracker needs.

Benchmarks and honest limits

PLM-8B is competitive with or ahead of open-data peers on video QA and sets the pace on the paper's own fine-grained benchmarks (which it also introduces — grain of salt). Against closed frontier models it trades blows on standard suites and wins mainly on detailed, temporally grounded video understanding, which is exactly the axis Meta curated data for. Nothing in the paper is evaluated on overhead or aerial imagery; every training source is web, instructional, or egocentric video. That is the gap we must measure ourselves.

Part II

Running it on aerial imagery

Four viable pipelines, ranked by value for effort in an offline analysis setting (recorded footage, workstation GPU, accuracy over latency). Rank order is the recommendation.

1
PLM grounding + SAM 2 propagation Recommended · zero training

PLM localizes the target from a text description on one (tiled, full-resolution) frame; the box prompts SAM 2, which propagates a tracked mask through the clip. This is the paper's own data-engine pipeline, inverted for inference. Re-query PLM every N seconds to acquire objects that enter the scene.

Effort DaysTraining NoneStrength Open-vocabulary, promptable, pixel-accurate masksRisk Domain gap on grounding
2
PLM as the analysis layer on existing tracks

Keep whatever tracker you have; feed clips plus a tracked region into PLM's RCap/RTLoc/RDCap-style prompts to classify behavior and answer questions per track ("when does this vehicle stop?"). This is the capability no other open model offers and it composes with Option 1.

Effort DaysTraining NoneStrength Semantic per-track analysisRisk Low
3
PE Spatial + detector head + ByteTrack Upgrade path

Fine-tune a detection head (Mask R-CNN / DETA) on VisDrone or UAVDT with PE Spatial as backbone, then run tracking-by-detection. Best small-object accuracy and throughput; the right move once Option 1's zero-shot quality is measured and found wanting.

Effort Weeks (training + data)Training Detector fine-tuneStrength Production-grade aerial MOTRisk Highest up-front cost
4
PLM per-frame detection + IoU/Kalman association

Run PLM grounding on every frame and link boxes yourself. Works, but a full VLM forward per frame is slow even offline, and VLM boxes are not detector-grade on tiny targets. Only worth it for very low frame-rate imagery (e.g. satellite passes).

Effort DaysTraining NoneStrength Simple mental modelRisk Slow, jittery boxes

Worked example — Option 1, end to end

Everything below runs offline on one GPU (PLM-3B fits in 24 GB; PLM-8B and SAM 2.1-Large are comfortable on an A100/H100). Snippets are illustrative — check the repos for exact current APIs.

Setup — one environment, three checkpoints
# PerceptionLM + Perception Encoder
git clone https://github.com/facebookresearch/perception_models.git
cd perception_models && pip install -e .

# SAM 2 (separate repo, separate checkpoint)
git clone https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2.git
cd sam2 && pip install -e .

# Checkpoints: facebook/Perception-LM-{1B,3B,8B} on HuggingFace;
# sam2.1_hiera_large.pt from the sam2 repo's download script.
Step 1 — PLM grounds the target on a full-resolution frame
from transformers import AutoProcessor, AutoModelForImageTextToText
import cv2

MODEL = "facebook/Perception-LM-8B"
processor = AutoProcessor.from_pretrained(MODEL)
model = AutoModelForImageTextToText.from_pretrained(MODEL, device_map="cuda")

# Grab frame 0 of the aerial clip. The IMAGE path tiles up to 36×448²,
# so small targets survive — never send full frames down the video path.
frame = cv2.cvtColor(cv2.VideoCapture("flight_042.mp4").read()[1],
                     cv2.COLOR_BGR2RGB)

conversation = [{"role": "user", "content": [
    {"type": "image", "image": frame},
    {"type": "text",
     "text": "Provide the bounding box of the white pickup truck "
             "driving on the dirt road."},
]}]
inputs = processor.apply_chat_template(conversation, return_tensors="pt",
                                       add_generation_prompt=True).to("cuda")
out = model.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=64)
box = parse_box(processor.decode(out[0]))   # → [x1, y1, x2, y2] in pixels
Step 2 — SAM 2 turns the box into a track
from sam2.build_sam import build_sam2_video_predictor

predictor = build_sam2_video_predictor("sam2.1_hiera_l.yaml",
                                       "sam2.1_hiera_large.pt")
state = predictor.init_state(video_path="flight_042_frames/")

# Seed with PLM's box on frame 0 → object id 1
predictor.add_new_points_or_box(state, frame_idx=0, obj_id=1, box=box)

# Propagate: a mask per object per frame — the actual track
tracks = {}
for f_idx, obj_ids, masks in predictor.propagate_in_video(state):
    tracks[f_idx] = {oid: (m > 0).cpu().numpy()
                     for oid, m in zip(obj_ids, masks)}

# Offline loop: every ~5 s, re-run Step 1 on a fresh frame, add any box
# that doesn't overlap an existing masklet (IoU < 0.3) as a new obj_id.
Step 3 (Option 2) — PLM analyzes the finished track
# Crop a 32-frame clip around track #1 (crop ≈ 2× the masklet's extent,
# so the 448² video path keeps the target large) and ask RDCap-style:
question = ("This clip follows a single vehicle. Describe its movements "
            "step by step, and state the frame ranges where it is stopped.")
# → "The truck travels north along the road (frames 0–210), slows and
#    stops beside a structure (frames 210–380), then continues north."

Future work

  1. 1
    Aerial evaluation set before anything else.

    Label 20–50 short clips from our own footage (boxes + identities). Measure PLM grounding recall on frame crops and Option-1 end-to-end HOTA/MOTA. Every later decision hangs on these numbers.

  2. 2
    Tiled-grounding utility.

    A small wrapper that splits a 4K aerial frame into overlapping crops, runs PLM grounding per crop, and merges boxes (NMS). Directly attacks the small-object weakness with no training.

  3. 3
    Re-acquisition & ID management.

    Periodic PLM re-grounding to catch entrances; IoU-gated matching against live SAM 2 masklets; track termination on mask collapse. This turns the demo into a multi-object system.

  4. 4
    Fine-tune if (and only if) step 1 says so.

    Two escalation levels: PLM grounding fine-tune on VisDrone-format data; or the full Option-3 build — PE Spatial + detector head + ByteTrack — for production-grade small-object MOT.

  5. 5
    Per-track analytics on top.

    Batch RDCap/RTLoc-style queries over finished tracks: stop detection, interaction events, route summaries — the layer that feeds trajectory planning downstream.

  6. 6
    Track the model line.

    SAM 2.1 already supersedes the SAM 2 used in the paper's data engine; watch the perception_models repo for PLM/PE successors and for community aerial fine-tunes.
